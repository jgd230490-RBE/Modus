Built around the plans you already have to write.

Construction logistics is already governed: by CLOCS, by FORS, by your client's carbon requirements and by the local planning conditions. Wayscope doesn't add another standard. It produces the numbers those standards ask for, from one plan.

Principal contractors and clients

A CLP that stays live.

The CLOCS Standard asks principal contractors for a Construction Logistics Plan that risk-assesses and specifies the safest vehicle routes, defines the last-mile routes to and from site, and stays a live document, reviewed before every new phase. Wayscope keeps those routes, the vehicle numbers behind them and the permitted deviations in one place. The plan you approve each week is the plan you share.

Gate checks, vehicle and driver accreditation and collision records stay in your delivery management system and site procedures. Wayscope plans the movements those systems then check.

Hauliers and fleet managers

Distance and emissions, by vehicle type.

FORS asks operators to use routes that keep distance and fuel down, to record the distance travelled by vehicle type, and, at Silver and Gold, to report emissions performance. Wayscope gives each haul its routed distance for the actual vehicle class, the trips per day and the CO₂e per trip, so planned and recorded figures can be compared like for like.

The figures are planned and modelled. Your fuel records and the FORS Emissions Calculator remain the operator's evidence.

Tier 1 suppliers and carbon leads

Transport carbon in the form the Carbon Tool asks for.

On major projects, suppliers complete the National Highways Carbon Tool every month. Transport goes in as quantity and one-way distance, per origin and destination, which is exactly how Wayscope holds every haul (transport to site, module A4 in PAS 2080 terms). The same plan supports the seven-day roadworks horizon that RIS3 reports against.

Prepared for entry into the Carbon Tool. National Highways has not reviewed or endorsed Wayscope.

Highways, planning and streetworks officers

Construction traffic you can see coming.

Construction Traffic Management Plans set HGV routes, delivery hours and limits on the local network. Wayscope shows which roads carry the movements, how many and when, with route caps (for example, a village limit per day) checked before the week is committed. Officers get a read-only map instead of a monthly spreadsheet. If extraordinary traffic ever becomes a conversation, everyone starts from the same record of what used which road.

Wayscope does not issue permits or replace Street Manager.

Logistics and construction managers

Logistics planned as part of the programme, not after it.

The professional bodies make the same case: plan logistics early, plan it with the supply chain, and measure how it performs. Wayscope sizes the fleet from cycle times, shows the effect of an artic against an 8-wheeler, puts a modelled fair-price floor beside each quote, and keeps planned against delivered on one screen.

CILT and CIOB guidance, applied to the haul: plan early, plan with the supply chain, measure the result.

Five problems it takes off the desk

Seven-day accuracy

Commit the week, then hit it.

The look-ahead commits next week's movements by day, then records what actually happened. The disruption you declared is the disruption that occurs, and the record shows it. RIS3 measures roadworks planned seven days ahead against what happens on the ground.

Routes, CTMPs and CLPs

Every haul route mapped, every restriction checked.

Routing is HGV-legal for the vehicle's real dimensions and weight. Planned closures come from National Highways' public feed. Route caps and seasonal windows are checked before the week is committed, and the read-only map serves the client and the community.

Carbon returns

Carbon from the numbers you planned with.

Each origin and destination carries its quantity and one-way distance, with routed kilometres, trips and CO₂e per trip on the 2026 DESNZ factors. Daily and to-date carbon sit on the map, and the figures are already in the form the Carbon Tool asks for.

Fleet sizing

How many lorries, not how many loads.

Vehicle numbers come from the movements and the trips one vehicle can make on that route's cycle, per vehicle class. Switching to an artic instead of an 8-wheeler changes the answer, and fewer vehicles means less exposure on the road.

Haulage cost

A fair price before the negotiation.

A modelled floor per trip, worked out from the weekly diesel index, distance, tonnes and cycle time. Typed quotes and target rates sit beside it. It is never called the cost.

Sits beside what you already run.

Wayscope takes quantities from your bill of quantities and dates from your programme, as a spreadsheet, and gives movements, routes, cost and carbon back the same way. It plans and records movements; it doesn't follow vehicles on the road, book delivery slots or run gate checks. Your delivery management system and site procedures keep doing that. It isn't a project-management suite either, and doesn't try to be.
