Booking tells you who's coming tomorrow. Planning tells you whether next month fits.

On highways and rail schemes, lorry movements are agreed long before the first delivery is booked: in the CLP, the CTMP, the carbon return and the conditions behind them. The plan then lives in a spreadsheet per stakeholder, and problems show up on the road.

  • Peaks from two work fronts land on the same road on the same day.
  • A route that suits a car is the wrong route for an 8-wheeler.
  • A few hundred metres per trip, over thousands of bulk loads on a long corridor, is real money.
  • Suppliers get next week's commitments by phone and email, with nothing to point back to.
  • Carbon returns get rebuilt each month from estimated distances.
  • Councils answer residents from a monthly spreadsheet.

Walkthrough 1

Set the places and the roads once.

Set up your quarries, site compounds, tips, railheads and stockpiles as locations, then add the materials and vehicles you actually run. Every haul is routed for the lorry's real dimensions, weight and axles: laden at gross, empty at tare. If a route hasn't been routed yet, it carries no distance, cost or carbon; nothing is estimated in its place. On bulk movements down a long corridor, a small distance error repeated on every trip becomes a large cost, so Wayscope uses the distance the lorry will drive.

Read the narration

On a long linear scheme, a planner moves thousands of loads between quarries, compounds and stockpiles. A standard map route assumes a car; a loaded forty-four tonne artic needs roads that take its size and weight. In Wayscope, you set your locations, vehicle types and materials once, on one map. Each vehicle type has its real dimensions: four metres high, sixteen long, five axles. Each haul is then baked for that vehicle, laden out and empty back, so the distance is the one the lorry really drives: nineteen miles here. Materials carry their density, and closures and haul roads are drawn as zones that routes avoid or use. That distance then feeds the forecast, vehicle numbers, cost and carbon estimate. Wayscope. Walkthrough 2

Forecast together. Find the clash before the week is confirmed.

Each team submits its own forecast submission, and the planner sees every line together before approving it. Approved lines feed the Look-ahead. Before a week is confirmed, the clash rail flags teams sharing a route or a road, routes running over their planning flag, National Highways planned closures and lines not yet routed. It flags, never blocks, so you can bring the impact down while it is still a plan. Then the plan goes out as a 4-week look-ahead and a 1-week commitment: you Confirm week, and suppliers get a PDF commitment sheet with a route map.

Read the narration

On a linear scheme, every team plans its own haulage. Wayscope brings those forecasts together. Each team enters its lines: origin, destination, material, one vehicle and monthly quantities. The planner sees every line in one list, and approves or rejects each one, with a reason. Approved lines feed the Look-ahead: this week to commit, the weeks after to make ready. Before a week is confirmed, the clash rail flags shortages, stockpiles over capacity, closures on routes in use, and routes above their planning flag. It flags; it never blocks. Afterwards, teams type what actually moved, and each line shows as held or short. The week goes out as a PDF commitment sheet with a route map, or as an XLSX export. Wayscope. Walkthrough 3

Every approval in one place.

The approved plan goes on a shared public map that opens with one password you hand out. It shows road by road and council by council, month by month: which roads carry the lorries, how many, and when. Road load adds up every route that shares a stretch of road, so you can see where it gets busy. Send the link or QR code to whoever needs it: the client, the highway authority, the parish council, the community liaison team.

Read the narration

Once the plan is approved, the same figures go onto one shared map. It shows two-way movements a day, vehicles needed and transport carbon, from the approved plan only. Six preset views answer the usual questions. Councils: movements shows lorry movements in each council area, month by month. Busiest roads adds up every route that shares a stretch of road, and the heat map shows volume or carbon. National Highways planned closures appear as an advisory layer. Every view has a link and a QR code, and the map works on a phone. Wall mode loops through the months on a large screen. It opens with one shared password you hand out. Wayscope. CLOCS

A CLP that stays live.

The CLOCS Standard asks principal contractors for a Construction Logistics Plan with risk-assessed routes, defined last-mile routes and a review before each new phase. Wayscope holds the routes, the vehicle numbers behind them, the 4-week look-ahead and each 1-week commitment in one approved plan, so the figures in your CLP and the week your suppliers receive come from the same place.

What stays with you: the CLP itself, gate checks, traffic marshals and your delivery management system.

FORS

Routes and distances by vehicle type.

FORS asks operators at Bronze, Silver and Gold for safe and efficient routes set to vehicle weight and dimensions, and for distance recorded by vehicle type. Wayscope gives each haul its routed distance for the vehicle that runs it, laden and empty legs separately, and the confirmed week goes out as a PDF commitment sheet before the lorries leave the yard.

What stays with you: fuel records and the FORS Emissions Calculator remain the operator's evidence.

National Highways

The inputs the Carbon Tool asks for.

The Carbon Tool records transport as quantity, mode and the single one-way distance from supplier to site. Wayscope holds the inputs the Carbon Tool asks for, per haul, from the first forecast. Transport carbon is a planning estimate on DESNZ 2026 factors, not a Carbon Tool return, and your Carbon Tool return stays your own. Each 1-week commitment is confirmed, then recorded as held or short, which supports the seven-day planning horizon in RIS3.

What stays with you: your Carbon Tool return.

Local authorities

The CTMP, on a map.

A CTMP sets designated HGV routes, delivery hours and caps on the local network. Enter a cap as a route's planning flag (maximum vehicles a day), and a week that goes over it shows up in the clash rail before it is confirmed. Officers get the shared public map by road and by council, month by month: how many lorries through the village a day, from the approved plan rather than a monthly spreadsheet.

What stays with you: permits, licences, s278 agreements, TROs and Street Manager. Wayscope does not issue or replace any of them.

A planning layer, not a booking system.

Wayscope sits on top of the delivery booking system you already run. It works out the forecast, the routes, the 4-week look-ahead and the 1-week commitment; your booking system takes the slots and your site team runs the gate. Teams enter their forecasts in Wayscope. There is no import today; where a scheme needs one from its booking system, it is scoped with that client during onboarding. Forecasts export as CSV, the confirmed week as XLSX and as a PDF commitment sheet with route map. Actuals are typed in, as held or short, not counted or tracked.

Built around the documents you already write.

Construction logistics on highways and rail schemes is already governed. Wayscope doesn't add a standard; it holds the figures these documents ask for, in one approved plan.

  • The CLOCS Standard: Construction Logistics Plan, principal contractor requirements 5.1–5.5
  • FORS Standard v7.2: O1 Routing, O2 Emissions
  • National Highways Carbon Tool and PAS 2080 (module A4, transport to site)
  • RIS3 performance specification: seven-day roadworks horizon
  • CTMPs and planning conditions set by local highway and planning authorities

These are named to describe what each one asks for and where Wayscope's figures fit. Wayscope is independent of every organisation named here, and none of them has reviewed Wayscope. Wayscope's carbon figures are planning estimates, not a Carbon Tool return.
