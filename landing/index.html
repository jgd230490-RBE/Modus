Five problems it takes off the desk

Seven-day roadworks accuracy

Commit the week, then hit it.

The look-ahead commits next week's movements by day and records what actually happened, so the disruption declared is the disruption that occurs. RIS3 measures roadworks planned seven days ahead against what happens on the ground.

Haul routes, TMPs and CLPs

Every haul route mapped, every restriction checked.

HGV-legal routing with the vehicle's real dimensions and weight. Planned closures from National Highways' public feed. A read-only corridor map for the client and the community, without giving them an account.

Monthly carbon returns

Carbon from the numbers you planned with.

The Carbon Tool wants transport as quantity and one-way distance. Wayscope holds both per origin and destination, with routed kilometres, trips and CO₂e per trip, on 2026 DESNZ factors. Daily and to-date carbon sit on the map.

[EXPLAINER 4: vehicles from cycle time, artic against 8-wheeler]

Fleet sizing

How many lorries, not how many loads.

Vehicles come from the movements and the trips one vehicle can make on that route's cycle, per vehicle class. An artic instead of an 8-wheeler changes the answer.

Haulage cost

A fair price before the negotiation.

A modelled floor per trip from the weekly diesel index, distance, tonnes and cycle time. Typed quotes and target rates sit beside it. It is never called the cost.

[EXPLAINER 5: fair-price floor beside a quote]

Sits beside what you already run.

Takes quantities from your bill of quantities and dates from your programme export, as a spreadsheet. Puts movements, routes, cost and carbon back the same way. It is not a project-management suite and does not try to be one.
