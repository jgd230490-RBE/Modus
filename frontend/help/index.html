User guide v2.0 · 16 September 2026 · back to the app · route map

1. Welcome

Modus is a shared planning platform for construction haulage on linear infrastructure programmes — rail, road and utility corridors. Every delivery team forecasts its material movements into it, planners approve them, and the week's haulage commitment — how much moves on which route, on which day, with how many trucks — comes out of it. A route map shows where the hauls run, what is in the way on the roads where the country's road authority publishes it, and which works package owns each stretch of the alignment.

Your organisation's words

This guide says team for a delivery team. Your organisation may call them packages, contractors or IPTs — the app uses your organisation's word and its own team names, and shows money in your organisation's currency. Where this guide shows a currency it is only an example.

This guide is for the people who use the platform: team submitters, planners and anyone who reads the route map. It explains each screen, what to type, what the numbers mean and where they come from. It deliberately leaves out internal names — the technical guide has those.

Access codes are shared per role and checked by the server on every request. A team code shows you only that team's lines, wherever you look. Per-person logins with passwords are planned as the next security phase; until then treat your code as you would a shared key.

1.3 How the numbers are built — in one picture

Everything you type is a quantity per month on one route for one material and one vehicle type. The platform converts it to tonnes using the material's density, to trips using the vehicle's payload, and to vehicles using how many trips one truck can make in a 10-hour shift on that route's measured cycle. Distances and drive times come from truck routing for the exact vehicle, out laden and back empty. Section 9 explains each step and what "planning figure" means.

2. Getting started

2.1 Signing in

  • Open the site address and press Staff sign in at the top right.
  • Type your name and your access code. Your name is stamped on the lines you submit and the weeks you confirm, so use the name your colleagues know.
  • The code is kept for this browser tab only; closing the tab signs you out. Your name and your last page are remembered on this computer.
  • If the platform says the code is not recognised, check for a trailing space and that you have the current code — codes are rotated by the planners.

2.2 Finding your way around

The left rail is grouped: Plan (Dashboard, Submit forecast, Forecasts), Track (Look-ahead), Data (Locations, Routes, Zones, Config — planners and admins only) and Map (the route map inside the app). The rail can be collapsed to icons with the button at its foot; it remembers the page you were on. The Forecasts entry carries a badge with the number of lines waiting for approval when you are a planner.

  • Vehicle names can be shown in English trade names or as EU vehicle categories — the toggle in the header changes the label only; nothing stored changes. Where your organisation works in a country with local vehicle names, a third option appears; a vehicle with no local name falls back to the EU string with a star.
  • The header shows your role and, for a team code, your team's name. Sign out is in the header.
  • The app works in a desktop browser at 1440 px wide or more; the staff app has no phone layout. The route map does.

3. The Dashboard

The Dashboard is the strategic view of the forecast. Filter by status (Approved, Pending or all), unit, material, vehicle and month range. The six tiles read Total material, Total trips, Truck-km, CO₂e, Intensity and Peak month; below them are charts for material demand over time, vehicle trips over time, carbon over time, the material split and the top routes by tonnage, then a per-route table (Route planning detail) with the cycle time, trips per day and vehicles for each route and vehicle.

  • Truck-km, CO₂e and vehicles use the routed distance and cycle for each line's route and vehicle. A route that is not yet baked for that vehicle shows "not baked" rather than a figure.
  • Vehicles are trucks, not trips: peak demand per day divided by what one truck can physically do in a shift. Hover the tile for the rule.
  • The Dashboard does not calculate anything of its own; it reads the same figures the Look-ahead and the route map use, so the three cannot disagree.

Stockpile capacity shows how full each stockpile is at the end of a week — the weeks of this month and next are pills above the chart, the current one marked. A bar turns amber from 90 % and red past its recorded capacity; hover it for what came in and went out that week and the room left. Locations that hold stock but have no recorded capacity are listed beside the chart with their balance and "max —": no capacity recorded is not zero capacity. A week's closing balance is the opening quantity plus the week actuals typed for deliveries there, minus the consumption typed on the Look-ahead's Account view (§6.4); a week nobody reported counts as nothing. The panel is read-only.

4.1 The totals strip

As you type, the strip at the foot of the form updates: the total, the equivalent tonnes, average and peak movements per day (the month's loads divided by 22 working days), and vehicles needed (average and peak) — the movements per day divided by the trips one truck can make in a shift on this route with this vehicle. That last figure needs the route to be baked for the vehicle; if it is not, the strip says "not baked" and nothing is estimated in its place. Tell a planner: baking is a one-click action on the Routes page.

4.2 Warnings you may see

  • Seasonal restriction — where your configuration defines a seasonal window (for example a spring-thaw season when axle-weight limits are likely on secondary roads), a quantity in that window on a restricted vehicle shows the window's warning. It warns; it does not block or re-route. With no windows configured, nothing is shown.
  • Not baked — the route has no measured geometry for this vehicle yet. You can still submit; the line simply carries no distance or vehicle figures until a planner bakes it.
  • Caution on save — another line already exists for this route and month (for example a second discipline). Both stay; the caution makes sure you know.

4.3 Saving, editing and withdrawing

Save writes every month in the range; a cell left at 0 removes that month. The line appears on the Forecasts page as Pending until a planner approves it. To change it later, open the line from the Forecasts page with Edit — the whole year range reopens in this form. Withdraw removes the whole line across every year it covers; the confirmation tells you the span and the number of months.

5. The Forecasts page

Every forecast line you are allowed to see, grouped per route, discipline and work section across every year it covers. Filter by status (the count chips are clickable), team, discipline, "mine only" and free text. Export CSV downloads what is on screen.

5.1 For planners: approving and rejecting

  • Approve and Reject act on the line (route, discipline and section), not on the whole route. A rejection needs a reason — the form refuses an empty one — and the reason is shown to the submitter.
  • Approving a month creates its weeks in the Look-ahead immediately and puts the line on the route map. Reopening or rejecting an approved month later does not delete weeks that already carry typed actuals; they simply stop refreshing and show "month is now Pending".
  • Approval does not check that the route is baked for the vehicle. The Look-ahead will flag an unbaked line (UNBAKED) and show trips only. Bake before you approve, or straight after.

6. The Look-ahead

The Look-ahead turns an approved monthly forecast into a weekly plan, and the week that contains today into a daily commitment sheet for hauliers. It has three views, switched with the pills under the page title: Account (last week — what actually happened), Commit (this week, or next week — the plan by day) and Horizon (the coming weeks as totals). It opens on Commit.

6.1 How weeks work

  • A week runs Monday to Sunday and belongs to the month that holds its Thursday — ISO 8601 week numbering. A month therefore has four or five weeks; September 2026 has four, October and December five.
  • An approved month's quantity is split equally over its weeks, and each week equally over Monday to Friday. Saturday and Sunday default to zero.
  • Anything you type over — a week or a day — stops following the split and is marked as typed. If the month is later re-approved with a different quantity, typed weeks keep their values and show PARENT CHANGED rather than silently moving.

6.2 The weekly rhythm

6.3 Commit view — this week by day

The header shows the week's dates. The KPI strip reads: planned this week (tonnes), trips, vehicles per day at the peak, t·km, planned cost (or "no rate typed"), last week's shortage, and last week delivered: n of m lines — a line counts as delivered when the actual reached 98 % of the plan.

The clash rail under the strip lists flags for the week — up to three, then "+N more". Flags never block anything; they tell you what to look at (§10 lists them all). The most important for a team is SHARE: another team is on the same route, or the same origin and destination, on the same day. You are told you share it — by the other team's name — without seeing their quantities.

Each row of the grid is one line: origin → destination, a team chip, a work-section chip, the material and the vehicle. The Monday–Friday cells show the quantity and, beneath it, trips · vehicles for that day — for example 17 tr · 3 veh. Today's column is tinted.

  • Click a row to see km per trip, km per day, km per vehicle, t·km, the cycle time and the cost of the line — or "rate not set" if no rate has been typed on the route.
  • Type over a day when the site's plan differs from the even split. The cell shows a typed marker and the day no longer follows the week total. If the days then no longer add up to the week, the line shows DAYS ≠ WEEK — a flag, not an error; you can still confirm.
  • Unbaked line — a route with no measured geometry for the vehicle shows trips but veh —, and the flag UNBAKED. Nothing is estimated in its place; ask a planner to bake the route.
  • The week map under the grid draws this week's lines in their team colour, origin dot navy and destination dot red; hover a line for its quantity and trips. A line with no geometry is listed under the map rather than drawn as a straight line.

Confirming the week

Confirm week (the green button, top right) is one act for the whole week: every planned cell locks, actuals stay typeable, and the sheet becomes the commitment. The dialog names the week and the number of lines and has four free-text notes — weather, wetness, traffic, other — for the record. Confirm remaining lines appears when some lines are already confirmed. A confirmed week can be re-opened to change the plan; nothing is deleted.

This week / next week

On Thursday you may want to commit next week while this week is still running. Switch the pill to Commit · next week: its days are created on demand and can be typed and confirmed in the same way. A shortfall spread on Thursday with a partial actual and again on Friday with the full figure carries only the difference — the platform never counts the same shortfall twice.

Exporting for hauliers

  • Export XLSX — a workbook with one row per line per day (quantity, tonnes, trips, vehicles, km, t·km, cost, cycle and its source, day status), a Clashes sheet and an About sheet. Weekdays only.
  • PDF — the one-week commitment sheet: landscape, a route map on the first page, one row per line sorted by origin, Monday to Friday columns with quantity and trips tr · veh, origin and destination with coordinates, the week total, km per trip and the cost where a rate is typed, then the flags and the footer caveats. It says CONFIRMED only when every line is. It is a commitment sheet, not a delivery note: payloads are planning figures, not plated weights, and km come from truck routing.
  • If the PDF's map is a drawn schematic rather than a photo map, the caption says the map service could not be reached; the sheet is otherwise identical.

6.4 Account view — what actually happened last week

Account shows last week per line: planned, an actual box, the variance (green when over-delivered, red when short; an empty actual means "not reported", never zero), an actual cost box for the invoiced or agreed cost of the week, a note, and the status of the shortfall — held, carried or remaining.

  • Type the actual quantity as it was delivered. Saving an actual never changes next week's plan by itself.
  • spread on this week carries a shortfall into the commit week, spread evenly over the remaining weekdays from today. Press it once the figure is known; pressing it again after a correction carries only the difference. It refuses if the target week is already confirmed — re-open the week first.
  • Actual cost is the first place the platform records money spent rather than planned; the KPI strip compares it with the planned cost where rates exist.
  • The Stockpiles block at the foot lists each stockpile for the account week: opening balance, inbound (from the typed actuals on routes delivering there — a route nobody reported counts as nothing), an out box for what was consumed, the closing balance and the remaining capacity. Type the consumption once a week; the Dashboard's Stockpile capacity panel and the route map's stockpile gauges read it.

6.5 Horizon view

Horizon shows week totals for this month and the next — four or five columns per month, each headed with its Monday–Sunday dates — with the commit week highlighted. Nothing is confirmed here; it is the look-ahead in the ordinary sense: what is coming, so make-ready and early warning can happen.

Good habits

Open the Look-ahead before a meeting: the page reads a lot of data and can take several seconds to load on first open.

Type actuals on Monday, confirm the week once the grid is right, export the PDF for hauliers, and use the next-week switch on Thursday.

Read the clash rail before confirming. A ROUTE CAP or STOCKPILE OVER flag will not stop you — it is a planner's call.

7. Data pages (planners and admins)

Locations, Routes and Zones share one map; switching between them keeps your view. Writes on these pages need the admin token typed into the box on the page (it is remembered in this browser). Paste it into the box, never into the address bar.

7.1 Locations

  • Add a location by clicking on the map in add mode, then complete the form: name, type (Quarry, Port, Compound, Site, Railhead, Stockpile, Other), what it supplies and receives (material categories — a route can only be created between an origin that supplies and a destination that receives the same category), operator and detail.
  • Drag a marker to move a location; every route using it is re-routed automatically.
  • Gates — a location can have several. Each has a direction (access, egress or both), a default flag, and two planning times: induction (safety briefing, applied once per arrival at that gate) and gate-to-face travel (ignored on routes that use a drawn haul road, which carries its own time). Routes start and end at gates, not at the site marker. A route can name a specific gate at either end; otherwise the location's default gate is used. A deactivated gate that a route still names stops that route from routing until the route is re-pointed.
  • Stockpile capacity — for stockpiles and other storage, record the capacity (tonnes or m³) and the opening quantity. Without a capacity a stockpile can never show as over; the map draws it with a dashed outline to say so.
  • Deleting a location cascades to its routes; the confirmation says what goes.

7.2 Routes

  • Create a route from an origin and destination (only valid pairs are offered; the id is assigned as R001, R002 …). Set the material category and, if the route belongs to one team, the team.
  • Bake — pressing Bake asks the routing service for the route as driven by each ticked vehicle: out laden and back empty, both as a truck of that weight, height and axle count. "Baked" is per vehicle; the chips show fully baked (green) or outbound-only (amber). Bake all runs the whole network in batches; the cost estimate counts calls, and a route with a temporary haul road costs more.
  • Route analysis row — km and drive time per leg, turnaround, cycle time, trips per day, tonnes per day and per month for each vehicle. A ‡ marks a cycle that includes a gate induction figure or an estimated return leg.
  • Alternatives — the routing service returns up to three options; the extra ones are shown when a route is selected. Use this option promotes an alternative to be the primary. A promotion is lost when the route is next baked (a moved location, an edited endpoint, a zone change, a vehicle change or Bake all).
  • Vehicles — tick or untick the vehicles a route is baked for. The confirmation names the forecast lines that use each vehicle and warns when the last vehicle would go.
  • Edit origin, destination, material or team in place. The impact dialog shows what will change — forecasts and weeks are kept; moving an endpoint clears the cached geometry and that end's gate, and the affected vehicles are re-baked.
  • Planning · Look-ahead block — the optional figures the Look-ahead prices and checks against: max vehicles per day (a planning cap that raises ROUTE CAP, never a block), rate per load, rate per tonne, rate per km (the three add up — type what the haulier actually quotes, typically a base per load plus a per-km rate), and the km basis for the per-km rate and t·km (round trip, the default, or loaded leg only). Nothing is pre-filled; a route reads "rate not set" until you type one.
  • Road restrictions — only where a restriction provider is connected for your organisation's country (today: Estonia's Tark Tee). check now runs the route against the authority's live restriction data in the background and stores the result with its time; the Look-ahead and the exports show the hits and their age. A re-bake clears the stored check. Elsewhere the card is not shown.
  • Diagnostics card — "Check factors" confirms every vehicle is matched to its configuration entry; "Probe" runs a live routing call for one route and shows what the service returned.

7.3 Zones and temporary haul roads

  • Disruption zone — draw a polygon on the map, give it a name, a kind (closure, weight limit, works, other), an optional date window and whether it affects routing. When it does, its bounding box is sent to the routing service as an area to avoid, every route crossing it is re-routed, and it draws red on both maps; otherwise it is drawn grey for information. The panel prints the exact box that will be sent before you save. Because the service takes boxes, not shapes, a long diagonal zone blocks more than it should — draw several small ones. Deleting a zone does not put the old route back; deactivating is usually the better choice.
  • Temporary haul road — draw a line, give it an assigned speed, and attach it to routes from the route's expanded row (in order, if a route uses more than one). The routing service does not know a road cut last week exists; the platform routes to the road's end, uses your line at your speed, and routes on from the other end. The service's own time is kept alongside so the change is auditable. A road with no speed adds distance but no time and is flagged.
  • Zones and haul roads change routes, not forecasts: an approved forecast is not re-warned when its route gets longer. Look at the Dashboard or the Look-ahead after a change.

7.4 Config

Config holds every number the platform calculates from, on five tabs: Vehicles (gross weight, payload, kg CO₂e per km, load and unload minutes, routing dimensions, labels), Materials (density, default unit, which vehicles are allowed), Planning (working days per month, shift hours per day, fallback load and unload minutes), Seasonal restrictions (month windows, restricted vehicles, the message shown — none by default) and Everything (JSON) for anything the forms do not cover, including your organisation's settings: its name, its word for a team, its currency and its country (the country decides which road-restriction, diesel-price and orthophoto services apply). Save applies the whole document, everywhere, for everyone, immediately; Reset to file restores the defaults shipped with the software. The page says whether the live configuration currently differs from those defaults.

Config refuses some edits, deliberately

You cannot remove a vehicle that has baked routes (every figure on those routes would silently fall to a hidden default and read the same), set a gross weight below a payload, name a vehicle that does not exist in a material category or a seasonal window, or remove the default routing vehicle. The error lists the problems; nothing is stored until they are fixed.

8. The route map

The route map opens with your staff sign-in — signed in to the app, it opens with no further prompt. Someone outside your organisation needs the separate map password, which opens the map and nothing else. Open the site address followed by /map/, or use the Map entry in the staff app. The Control Panel on the left is grouped top to bottom: Route filtering, Basemap, Overlays, Haul routes, Road restrictions and Forecast traffic volume. On a phone the panel is closed by default; the ☰ button opens it.

8.2 Filters, basemaps and overlays

  • Route filtering — origin, destination, discipline and team. The filters apply to routes, the timeline callouts and the KPI panel. Filter to your team and the timeline shows your traffic only. Reset filters clears them.
  • Basemap — Light (default), Standard (3D), Dark and Satellite (Mapbox), plus a national orthophoto where one is connected for your country (today: Estonia's, from Maa-amet). Every overlay survives a basemap switch. 3D buildings are available on the vector basemaps only.
  • Overlays — Alignment (by package) with its team legend; Work section boundaries; Alignment (survey, continuous) — the raw survey with every track type, off by default; Chainage markers (thinned by zoom so the corridor is not a smear); Sites & assets with its legend; 3D buildings; Site gates; Disruption zones; Existing rail network.
  • Haul routes — Laden (out), Empty (return), Temporary haul, Alternative routes.
  • If the alignment legend reads "no alignment in this tenant", your organisation has not loaded an overlay package yet — an admin does that once; see Appendix D.

8.3 Road restrictions

This section applies only where a restriction provider is connected for your country; otherwise the Road restrictions group is not shown at all. Tick a layer under Road restrictions to draw it; the value (for example a 3.2 m height limit) is labelled from zoom 11. Click a restriction near a route for its details and the verdict against the vehicle — "3.2 m limit, vehicle 4.0 m, EXCEEDS by 0.8 m" — for mass, height and width. Weak bridges carry a load *class* rather than a tonnage, so their verdict is "unknown" with the class quoted. The data is live from the provider and refreshes itself every 30 minutes; only restrictions currently in force are shown. It is advisory: routes are not re-planned around it.

8.4 The forecast timeline and KPI panel

Under Forecast traffic volume, choose a month range and press ▶ Play forecast timeline. The map steps month by month through the approved forecasts: routes carrying volume are drawn, the sites in use get a white ring while everything else dims, small callouts name the busiest origins and destinations, and the KPI panel at the top left reads the month — Movements / day, Vehicles needed, Material / day and Active routes. Use the switcher under the cards to cut the month by Discipline, Team or Work section. ◀ and ▶▶ step a month; × closes the timeline. Forecast detail table opens the month-by-month matrix.

  • Vehicles needed is the sum, line by line, of movements per day divided by what one truck can do on that route's cycle — a route that is not baked is counted as unknown and the label says how many lines are unbaked. An empty month shows dashes, not zero.
  • At a wide zoom a busy month shows fewer callouts than there are sites: labels that would overlap are dropped, busiest first. Zoom in and they come back.
  • The timeline plays approved monthly forecasts. It is a plan, not a live feed.

8.5 Street View

Click a site mark and press Street View for the nearest imagery. "No Street View here" means none exists within 50 m; the map checks for imagery before it fetches a picture, so nothing is spent on empty lookups.

A ‡ means part of the cycle is estimated (a return leg not yet routed, or a gate figure).
Trips per day (one truck)a 10-hour shift ÷ the cycle time, rounded down.Because it rounds down, a small change in drive time can change it by a whole trip.
Vehicles neededmovements per day ÷ trips per day, rounded up — per line, then summed.Trucks, not trips. A route not baked for the vehicle gives no figure rather than a guess.
km, t·kmtrips × the routed km (both legs by default; the loaded leg only where the route's km basis says so); tonnes × the same km.Km come from the routing service for the primary route.
CO₂ekm × the vehicle's kg CO₂e per km.The factors are stated as a DEFRA/BEIS 2025 basis and have not been traced to the source document — planning figures.
Costtrips × rate per load + tonnes × rate per tonne + trips × km × rate per km — whichever rates are typed on the route, in your organisation's currency.No rate is pre-filled. Whether a rate includes VAT is whatever you typed. No exchange rate is ever applied.
Stock balanceopening + inbound (typed actuals on routes delivering to the stockpile) − consumed (typed on Account).A week nobody reported counts as nothing in; an unsized stockpile can never be over.
What "planning figure" means

Every quantity on a sheet is derived from typed forecasts and configured assumptions, not weighed loads or telematics. The supplier PDF says so in its footer: payloads are planning figures, not plated weights; km come from the routing service. Use the figures to plan fleets and spot clashes; do not present them as a delivery record.

10.2 Chips and marks

  • Team chip and WS chip on every Look-ahead row and Forecasts line — the team chip shows the team's name, and each team keeps one colour.
  • Typed marker on a day cell — this day no longer follows the week split.
  • ‡ on a cycle — an estimated part (return leg not routed, or a gate induction figure).
  • "no team" on a line — a line no team has claimed yet; planners only.
  • Red "not baked" on the Submit form and Dashboard — no geometry for the vehicle.

10.3 The route map's warning levels

When the timeline is playing, a stack above it can show up to three warnings for the month on screen: Clash — a stockpile past its capacity; Warning — a road restriction that a visible route's vehicle exceeds; Caution — a seasonal window your configuration defines, matching the month and vehicle. Each can be dismissed for that month. If the stack stays empty for a month you would expect to show something, the sources may not have loaded; reload the page.

A.3 What the platform does not do

  • It is not real-time and holds no vehicle positions; the timeline plays approved forecasts.
  • It has no time-of-day model: no delivery windows, gate booking or queueing. A daily route cap is compared with an average, not a schedule.
  • It does not enforce anything against a planner's judgement: every clash, cap and restriction is a flag.
  • It does not invent a distance or a fleet size for a route that has not been routed for the vehicle.

Appendix D — Technical reference (admins)

Visible to admins only. It answers “where does that number come from?” in a meeting.

This is a summary, not the full technical guide

The complete technical guide runs to about fifty pages and is held separately as a Word document. What follows is the part people ask for most. Where the two disagree, the Word document is the one that has been reviewed.

D.1 How a haulage number is made

cycle          = drive out (laden) + drive back (tare) + turnaround
trips per day  = floor(shift hours / cycle)
trips needed   = ceil(tonnes / payload)
vehicles       = ceil(movements per day / trips per day)

Both drive legs are routed separately — the return at tare weight, so the way back is not assumed to be the way out reversed and different weight limits can apply. Turnaround is the vehicle's own load and unload minutes, plus gate induction, plus either gate-to-face time or a drawn haul road — never both.

Trips are not vehicles

A trip is one loaded run; a vehicle makes several a day. A fleet figure worked out as tonnes ÷ payload alone is a trip count, and overstates the fleet by whatever that multiple is — typically three to six times.

D.2 Which figures are sourced, and which are assumed

FigureStanding
Route distance and durationMeasured — returned by the routing service for a truck profile with that vehicle's weight and dimensions. Nothing in the platform invents a road.
PayloadsPlanning assumption. A typical carrying limit, not a plated maximum. It drives trips per tonne, so it is one of the two figures that most changes a fleet size.
Emissions (kg CO₂e per vehicle-km)Unaudited. Stated in the configuration as a DEFRA/BEIS basis; that provenance has never been traced to a document. If a client asks whether the carbon number is DEFRA's, the honest answer is that we believe so and have not checked.
Working days per month, shift hours per dayPlanning assumptions — and they decide every “trucks per day” figure in the platform. Check these two first when a fleet size looks wrong.
Diesel indexLive for EU countries — the EU Weekly Oil Bulletin price for the organisation's country, fetched server-side. The feed does not state whether the series is with or without taxes; it is taken as the taxed series and the widget says so. Typed everywhere else — an admin enters the weekly price, and every figure built on it says it was typed.
Fair priceA model, not a quote. Three of its coefficients have no public source and are labelled as assumptions. It reads as a floor for negotiation, which is what it is labelled as everywhere. It is never printed on a supplier's sheet.
Rail alignment and chainageTenant data — the overlay package the organisation supplies (an admin loads it once). Chainage in metres on one datum, which may start below zero. Its accuracy is the survey's: where a survey has no main-track geometry, a package boundary can have no line beneath it, and the map says so rather than drawing a confident colour.
Existing rail corridorOptional tenant data — as supplied in the overlay package, carrying the package's own caveat.
Demo organisationFictional. The demo package (a corridor in the English East Midlands) is invented end to end: names, quantities, rates and the alignment. It is not a real railway or a proposal for one.

D.3 The live sources, and how they fail

  • Truck routing — every baked distance and drive time. Its road graph lags the physical road, which on a construction programme matters: roads are realigned for years, and a route baked before a realignment can follow a road that is no longer there.
  • Road restrictions (where a provider is connected for the country) — cached, and stored per route with the date it was checked. Mass, height and width give a real verdict; a weak bridge gives a load class, not a tonnage, so it is reported unjudged. The whole layer is advisory. The source carries expired records.
  • Diesel price — for EU countries, refreshed in the background, never on the page read. A failed fetch keeps the last good figure and says when it failed. Elsewhere there is nothing to fetch: the price is typed.
  • Loading an organisation — an admin imports a whole organisation as one package file (POST /api/admin/tenant/import, into an empty organisation) and loads the map overlay (PUT /api/admin/overlay); GET /api/admin/tenant/export writes the organisation back out as one file. After an import, bake the network: imported routes read "not baked" until then.
  • Basemaps and Street View — optional. With no key the popups simply show no photo.
What leaves the platform

Normal operation sends site and route coordinates to the routing, mapping and restriction services, and a coordinate to Street View where it is used. No quantity, rate or forecast leaves. This is inherent to any routed map product — the alternative is not having routing — but it is a fair question and better answered before it is asked.

D.4 What must not be described as finished

Access is a shared code per role, checked by the server, plus a shared password for the map. There is no per-person identity, no rate limit and no audit trail of who changed what. Per-person logins are the next security phase. Do not describe the deployment as secure until they exist.

Some admin actions are additionally protected by a token. While that token is unset, those actions are open to anyone who can reach them.
