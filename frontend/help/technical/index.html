Technical guide

For the people who run Wayscope and answer “where does that number come from?”. The user guide says what a screen means and how to use it; the info boxes (ⓘ) on the screens say what each figure means in a sentence. This page holds what the screens leave out: the formulas in code terms, the reads behind each screen, how routing is stored, and the settings that live on the deployment.

The words on the screens

Since 13 Oct 2026 a screen does not say API, endpoint, server, JSON, cache, geometry, bake or token (except the admin token's own field, the admin panel behind Config → More and the “Everything (JSON)” tab; the Diagnostics panel left the screens on 22 Oct 2026, see What the screens leave out). Those words are used here. T6 maps the old screen words to the new ones.

T1 How each figure is made

Every planning figure on the Dashboard comes from one read, GET /api/costing/lines, which uses the same functions as the Look-ahead (backend/costlines.py). The browser formats; it does not work anything out. A forecast month (the screens used to say line-month) is one month of one forecast line: a route, a team, a section, a material and one vehicle.

Tonnes and conversions

The planned quantity of every forecast month in view, in tonnes through each line's conversion (m³ and vehicle loads included). The Dashboard card shows a compact figure (bigNum, e.g. 12.3k t); its info box shows the exact figure.

Trips and deliveries a day

trips (per forecast month)  = ceil(tonnes ÷ planning payload)        — rounded up per line-month
deliveries a day            = Σ trips ÷ (months in view × working days per month)

Working days per month is planning.working_days_per_month on Config → Planning (22 by default). Each delivery is a two-way movement. The Look-ahead and the Today panel use the same rule per day.

Distance and the cycle

lorry-km      = trips × the routed (baked) route distance, both legs, for the line's vehicle
cycle         = drive out (laden) + drive back (empty) + turnaround
turnaround    = the vehicle's load + unload minutes + gate induction + (gate-to-face OR a drawn haul road, never both)

The per-trip distance is on the route's basis: the round trip unless the route says loaded only. An unbaked line (no stored route for its vehicle) has no distance: it adds nothing to the km, CO₂e and fair totals, which say how many lines they exclude. It is excluded, never estimated. The load and unload minutes are the vehicle's own (Config → Vehicles, seeded from backend/factors.json); blank minutes use the planning defaults.

Vehicles and peak fleet

trips per vehicle-day = drivers_hours.lorry_day(shift, driving, other work)["trips"]   — 17 Oct 2026
vehicles (per line)   = trips per day ÷ trips per vehicle-day      — the server's `vehicles`
peak fleet            = the largest same-month sum of vehicles needed across the lines in view

Since 17 Oct 2026 the trips one vehicle makes in a day follow the UK drivers' hours (backend/drivers_hours.py, called by network.route_analysis, the public map's route figures and the Carbon planner — never a second formula): the largest n with n × driving ≤ 540 min (Reg 561/2006 Art 6) and n × cycle + the break ≤ the shift, the break 45 min once driving passes 270 min (Art 7) or work passes 540, else 30 min once work passes 360 (Road Transport (Working Time) Regulations 2005 reg 7) — the Art 7 and reg 7 breaks are one break, never added. No further break past 720 min of work: the 45 may be taken as 15 + 30 (Art 7; reg 7(4)), which lets every stretch of work be kept to 6 h (reg 7(1)) in a 13 h day — the 15 before the 30, placed to suit; where they fall is not modelled (18 Oct 2026; the first build's extra 15 min was stricter than the law). Driving = both legs as routed (haul roads and gate access roads are inside them) + gate-to-face minutes; other work = load + unload + the gate check-in (safety_minutes). One driver's day is read as at most 13 h (the 11 h daily rest); Config accepts a longer shift (a double-shifted lorry, a gate's stand-in window) and planning.shift_note says the trips use 13. An electric lorry's charging is counted as working time (the cautious reading: no rule says yet; DfT's 2024–25 zero-emission vans consultation asked operators, and its November 2025 response did not settle it): on top of the break and counted when the break is chosen (carbon.ev_day, drivers_hours.ev_trips); a day that holds no trip is day_fits False, not a battery failure. Each row carries drive_min, other_work_min and lorry_day (limited_by shift / breaks / driving / driver_day / none, the break, trips_without_rules = the old floor(typed shift ÷ cycle)). 0 trips = not one round trip fits a driver's day: every reader counts one lorry a trip (derived.day_figures, costlines, carbon.lorries_for, the CLP figures). Not modelled: weekly limits, night work, the twice-a-week 10 h driving day, the reduced daily rest, where the break falls, a second driver, the day's other duties, GB domestic rules. The gate figures keep the full shift (a gate stays open while drivers take breaks).

That is trips ÷ (working days × what one vehicle does in a driver's day). An unbaked line has no cycle, so it needs an unknown number of vehicles and is left out of the sum. Trips are not vehicles: a fleet worked out as tonnes ÷ payload alone is a trip count.

Delivered against plan

delivered t        = Σ the week actuals typed on Look-ahead → Account, through each line's payload (actual_tonnes)
delivered %        = delivered t ÷ planned t across every forecast month in view
on reported months = delivered t ÷ planned t on the forecast months that carry an actual

A day actual rolls into its week. A week or month nobody reported counts as nothing (not yet delivered); it is never assumed. The Today panel's “this week to date” counts line-days (one day of one line).

Carbon

CO₂e (t)      = km × the vehicle's per-km factor ÷ 1000
              — DESNZ 2026 kg CO₂e per vehicle-km (average laden, both legs, from Config)
kg CO₂e per t = tCO₂e × 1000 ÷ the tonnes on baked lines
tonne-km      = tonnes × the route's basis km, baked lines

The factor is set per vehicle on Config → Vehicles and stored per km whatever unit the tenant reads (miles are converted for display only). Tailpipe only, one average-laden factor on both legs. The Carbon planner's all-in figure (materials and waste as well) is a different total: see the user guide, section 6.

Planned cost

Planned £ = the route's typed rates (summed: per load, per tonne and per km), or the Config target rate where the route has none, marked “target”. A route with any rate of its own is priced from that alone; the two are never mixed. Per tonne, per trip and per km are the same total divided by the tonnes, trips and km the lines run.

Fair cost (the model)

fair / trip = fuel (litres from the vehicle class, tonnes carried and the live diesel index)
            + cycle hours × (driver + vehicle standing)
            + km × running
            × (1 + margin)

The coefficients are on Config → Costing · fuel. Winter months add the fair_price.winter_consumption_uplift_pct fuel uplift (8 % by default). The diesel index is the government weekly price for Great Britain or the EU Weekly Oil Bulletin for an EU country, refreshed in the background, or typed. With no index there is no fair price. Shown on every routed line as “fair (model)”: a floor for negotiation, never a quote, never mixed with a typed rate. Three coefficients have no public source and are labelled as assumptions. The starting coefficients come from factors.json: fair_price_by_country holds a set per country (Great Britain, in £), and fair_price is the set for every other organisation (an Estonian set, in EUR). A coefficient missing from the organisation's own block is read from its country's set, and the page offers the country's set in one click when the figures differ. Each coefficient's source is in the file beside it (the screen no longer says “see the file”): for Great Britain the driver rate is ONS Annual Survey of Hours and Earnings 2025 pay as quoted by DfT, with an assumed employer on-cost multiplier; vehicle standing, running and margin are assumptions (running cross-checked against RHA Cost Tables 2026).

Fuel index and fuel surcharge (BAF)

  • What the user guide leaves out (28 Oct 2026; the user: no supplier or data-set names in the user guide outside its source tables A.1 and A.2). “The UK Government's 2026 factors” are DESNZ's greenhouse gas conversion factors 2026 (HGV, average laden, per vehicle-km; material use and waste disposal); “the UK national average (2026)” on the Carbon planner is DESNZ 2026's average for the same lorry sizes; the Carbon planner PDF's own stamp reads “Wayscope planning estimate, DESNZ 2026 basis, not Carbon Tool figures”. “The Government's weekly road fuel prices” are DESNZ's. On the public map, “the government's lists of schools and of bus stops and stations, and a map that volunteers keep” are the Department for Education's Get Information about Schools, the Department for Transport's NaPTAN and OpenStreetMap; “the government's road safety figures” are DfT's road safety data (STATS19), released each late July. A.1 and A.2 still name DESNZ, DfT and Innovate UK (the Open Government Licence asks for the source to be named).
  • What Config and the fuel strip leave out (28 Oct 2026; the user: no supplier or data-set names on a screen). The fuel strip (Config, Look-ahead Commit and Account) says “Weekly UK pump price” for DESNZ's weekly road fuel prices (gov.uk) and “Weekly EU price” for the EU Weekly Oil Bulletin (FUEL_PROVIDER_WORDS; the provider's own label stays in /fuel-index and on the documents). Config → Costing · fuel's hints name no study: L/100 km per tonne carried “typically 0.4–0.95” is DfT's payload study; the rigid empty figure is the DESNZ 2026 factor (checked 9 Oct 2026); the artic empty figure is ICCT's 34 L/100 km laden less 0.4 × 26 t; the winter uplift's “cars use about 15 % more at −7 °C” is the US DOE (fueleconomy.gov, cold weather). The user guide's A.1 names lorry routing, the street-level photos and the base maps without the company: they are HERE Routing v8 (T2), Google Street View and Mapbox.
  • The index. Great Britain: DESNZ's weekly road fuel prices (ULSD pump price, incl. duty and VAT; the widget also shows it ex-VAT). EU countries: the EU Weekly Oil Bulletin. Refreshed in the background, never on a page read; an admin can press “Fetch this week's DESNZ price now” / “Get this week's price now”. A failed refresh keeps the last stored price (its error kept as last_error / last_attempt_at; the widget shows “update failed …; showing the last price” with the raw text on hover). The price reads “stale” once its bulletin date is more than 8 days old.
  • Typed index. Elsewhere, or with no country set, or when nothing is published, an admin types it with “Use this index”: the same index row, marked typed. It is the index, not the yard price.
  • Yard £/L is what the haulier pays at the yard; optional, never replaces the index.
  • BAF = (index ÷ base − 1) × fuel share, shown as a second figure beside each quote; the quote itself is never replaced. The base is locked by the first Confirm week that finds an index and no base; later confirms never move it. One base per organisation, not per haulier. “Reset the BAF base” clears it, and the next Confirm week locks a fresh one.

Gate arrivals

Read from GET /api/gates/arrivals?month=N (backend/gate_arrivals.py). The same trips rule, all teams summed at the gate a lorry arrives by: laden at the destination's entry gate, empty (coming to load) at the origin's. Arrivals a day = trips ÷ working days. The gap is the gate's open minutes ÷ arrivals, an average with no hourly peak. Red only when that gap is shorter than the gate's typed check-in; a gate with no check-in typed is never compared. A team code gets the totals and the team names, never another team's figure.

Since 14 Oct 2026 (backend/gate_hours.py, the one rule for every screen and export): a gate's open minutes are its typed open hours (a window that ends before it starts runs past midnight) — since 17 Oct 2026 a gate with no hours typed is open the default gate hours (planning.default_gate_hours, Config → Planning, 08:00–17:00 when not set; applied when read, never stored on the gate) — less the scheme's no-delivery peak bands (planning.peak_bands, Config → Planning) and the gate's own shut windows (shut_windows, at most four, each within one day, stored as 08:30-09:15,12:00-12:30) inside them, taken off as one set so an overlap counts once. A shut time wholly outside the gate's hours, or shut times and bands that leave it no open minute, are refused when typed (gate_hours.fit_problem); a Config save that changes the default gate hours or the bands is refused by the same rule, naming each gate it would break (config.gate_problems). A gate left with no open minute anyway (a package import) reads "No open time" everywhere: red with any arrival, capacity 0. The lorry shift (planning.shift_hours_per_day) is no longer any gate's day. Capacity a day = open minutes ÷ check-in minutes a lorry; the gap uses the same open minutes. Two steps: Queue likely (amber) at 85 % of capacity or more (NEAR_PCT), Over gate capacity (red) past it — the red rule above, renamed on screen. Open days (seven flags, Monday first) are tested on the Look-ahead's dated days only: a month figure is a working-day average. The reply carries near_per_day (85 % of capacity) for the chart's guide tick.

Since 16 Oct 2026 (backend/sim.py, the haulage simulation): each gate also carries its busiest hour, from an average working day of the month played lorry by lorry. Each approved line's trips ÷ working days is rounded down or up to whole loads (the day's total is the month's average day rounded; the gates are kept as close as they can be to the figures above, then the smallest change to each line, then the lowest route numbers). Each gate's arrivals are spread evenly over its open minutes, the peak bands and its shut windows taken off (a gate with no hours typed takes the default gate hours). No lorry is placed before its gate opens: each sets off its route's drive time earlier, so the first lorries set off before the opening (a concrete pour is typed, so its mixers can come while the gate is shut; they wait, and the reply says how many). The busiest hour is the clock hour with most arrivals; the gate can check in 60 ÷ its check-in minutes an hour. The same day, with a scenario (open hours, check-in, peak bands, a concrete pour), is GET /api/sim/day?month=N: one lane at each gate, first come first served; a lorry that comes while the gate is shut (before it opens, in a band or in one of its shut windows) waits for it to open, and one still waiting after the last open minute is checked in late and counted; the bottleneck is the first wait longer than the check-in. Nothing is saved. A window past midnight is refused.

28 Oct 2026 (simulation v2, task 0e): backend/sim_site.py runs the chain ON SITE for every site with areas (site_areas: activity unload / load / wash, materials and vehicles as JSON lists, service_minutes, bays, a Point or Polygon) or internal roads (zones.location_id, haul_mode = 'internal', never spliced into a public route): gate in (sim.queue) → drive → area → drive → wash → drive → gate out, each station a first-come- first-served queue with bays servers; the drive is the shortest path by time over the drawn roads (ends joined within 5 m, a station snapping within 60 m, else straight lines) at each road's speed or locations.site_speed_kph (8 km/h stood in and said). A gate closed_from–closed_to is inactive for gates.resolve(on=); the site's other gate serves both directions (closed_fallback); a month counts as closed when the closure covers more than half its weekdays. GET /api/sim/day takes scale=month|week|day (week=commit|next, date=YYYY-MM-DD; the week and day read forecast_days through days.list_days and derived.day_figures, each line's day rounded up to whole trips as the grid does), and the sites' what-ifs site_speed=LOC=kph, service=AREA=min, bays=AREA=n, wash=on|off. The reply adds sites[] (stations with the gate's figures, the paths for the drawing in every reply, no_area lines), each arrival's stops and hops, day_choices and checks.sites_add_up. /api/site-areas lists (any sign-in); the admin writes are /api/admin/site-areas.

29 Oct 2026 (the user: a scenario where lorries bottleneck at the gate): the scenario's arrival pattern. sim.place takes a pattern: even (D59, the default, as above) or rush — the first k = round(share × n) of a gate's arrivals, in the same phase order (every line's first trips first, the lines interleaved), evenly over the first W OPEN minutes (bands and shut times skipped), the other n − k evenly over the remaining O − W; W is capped at O, and W ≥ O or k = 0 is the even spread. Query: arrivals=even|rush, rush_share (1–100, %), rush_minutes (5–240; defaults 30 / 30, RUSH_DEFAULT_SHARE / RUSH_DEFAULT_MINUTES); a value outside its range is a 400 in words. The reply carries arrival_pattern {kind, share, minutes, words} and, per gate, rush_n (its lorries placed in the window) and first_trip_share — the plan's lorries at the gate (each line's loads a day ÷ the trips one lorry makes a day, rounded up, through derived.cycle_for) over its arrivals, null where a line at the gate has no cycle; the card prints it as a note. Every gate, every scale; the pour on top; the site chain (sim_site) follows the gate's check-in times. On screen only (D86): nothing is saved, and the Dashboard's Gate arrivals, the Look-ahead's GATE OVER / NEAR flags, the public map and the big screen keep the even spread. The figures are a what-if on a planning day, never a prediction.

Stockpiles

27 Oct 2026: the balance runs FORWARD from the plan (backend/stockpiles.py, balances(); John's decisions D1–D11 of 27 Oct 2026). Nothing is stored that it works out. Weeks before the current week are past, the current week and later are the forecast.

balance_end(W) = count(W) if a stock count is typed, else balance_end(W−1) + In(W) − Out(W)
In(W)   every APPROVED line whose route ends at the place, one line-week at a time:
          past + actual typed      -> the actual
          past + nothing typed     -> the plan (counted in not_recorded)
          forecast                 -> the week row's planned_qty (edited, confirmed, calibrated) or the month ÷ its 4 / 5 weeks
Out(W)  typed Out (stockpile_weeks.consumed_qty) -> that figure: everything that left, road hauls out included
        else -> planned use (stockpile_plan, the month ÷ its weeks) + approved lines whose route STARTS at the place
        a planned Out never takes more than the place holds; the rest is `short`, and the week is `empty` (≥ 1 unit)

Only a place with a capacity or a planned use runs forward (forecast); any other keeps the records-only rule (actuals in, typed Out out). Pending and Rejected lines never count; units go through _to_pile_unit. Per week the read returns basis (recorded · part · plan · forecast), in_basis, out_basis, plan_in, plan_out, in_recorded, not_recorded, short, empty, count, count_adjust and the week's Monday (from); per place first_over, first_empty, peak and the 8 weeks ahead (soon_over, soon_empty, AHEAD_WEEKS). One computation serves every reader of a page until the next write (_MEMO, keyed on db.data_version() and the current week).

Writes: PUT /api/stockpiles/plan (one month or from–to) and PUT /api/stockpiles/count, planners and admins; GET /api/stockpiles/plan. The rail: STOCKPILE_OVER (the commit week over capacity, red), STOCKPILE_SOON (over or runs out within 8 weeks, amber, not for a place with no plan) and STOCKPILE_EMPTY (runs out in the commit week, amber), on the week's lines into the place. /api/public/stockpile-timeline sends a month's over and a figure above the capacity to staff only; the public read is capped at the capacity with full (D9 a + i), kept by read_cache. The Dashboard chart is red past capacity and amber from 90 %; a place with no capacity recorded is named, never drawn as zero.

CLP monitoring

Read from GET /api/clp/monitoring?month=N (backend/clp.py), the first time the Dashboard section is opened; the CLP pack prints the same reply. Each chart comes back with state "set", or "empty" and missing in words. Every figure is the module's that owns it, never worked out again:

routes vs cap      bulletin.route_cap_rows: the month's approved loads ÷ working days vs max_vehicles_per_day
sites vs CLP cap   gate_arrivals.location_visits: laden + coming-to-load trips ÷ working days vs clp_cap_per_day
vehicle / Euro     carbon.scenario's baseline lines: trips by vehicle (equal to costlines' trips), Euro class from Config
councils           areas.area_load: districts, movements a day, laden and total lorry-km (÷ 1.609344 for miles)
carbon a tonne     all-in total_t × 1000 ÷ tonnes, as planned and with the target's levers; target_t (carbon_target.status) ÷ plan tonnes
trips avoided      the same scenario: baseline trips − trips with the target's levers; delta_vkm_km

The CLP pack is the same reply printed: GET /api/clp/pack.pdf?month=N and /api/clp/pack.xlsx?month=N (backend/clp_pack.py), exactly the charts in state "set", each with its table, basis and source; the rest listed under "Not drawn this month". It draws and never computes. The demo notice is on every page (the shared page frame) and heads every sheet.

Scope: the vehicle, carbon and trip charts are the caller's visible lines (a team code: its own, and its share of the target); the caps and councils are the scheme's. No figure is observed: no gate log is held (decision D42).

Abnormal loads (S16 / B5, 22 Oct 2026)

Each abnormal load is its own row in abnormal_loads (one tenant's, carried in its package), never a forecast line: nothing of it reaches the tonnes, trips, vehicles, CLP caps, gate capacity or carbon figures (D70, D71). The rules engine is backend/abnormal_rules.py, every threshold a named constant with its source, and test_abnormal.py asserts each on both sides.

  • When a load is abnormal. Over 44 t gross, over 2.9 m wide, over 18.65 m rigid, over 10 t on an axle (11.5 t driving): the Road Vehicles (Authorised Weight) Regulations 1998 and the Construction and Use Regulations 1986 as the National Highways aide memoire reads them. Construction and Use covers up to 4.3 m wide and 27.4 m long; STGO 2003 Schedule 1 gives Category 1 (up to 50 t on 5 or 6 axles), 2 (80 t, 6 or more) and 3 (150 t), with 12.5 / 12 / 16.5 t axle limits, 1.35 m minimum spacing and the tonnes-a-metre rule; over 150 t, 6.1 m wide or 30 m long is a Special Order (BE16). Width over 5.0 m (to 6.1 m) is a VR1.
  • Notices and lead times. Police: 2 clear working days over 3.0 m wide, 18.75 m rigid (25.9 m combination) or 80 t, 5 days where the aide memoire says so; road and bridge authorities: 2 days to 80 t, 5 over, with an indemnity; Network Rail where a railway structure is crossed. Special Order: the aide memoire's 10 weeks, the BE16 form's 8 shown beside it (plan on 10); VR1: 2 weeks (NPCC 2025: the 30-day window). Notify by is the calendar day before the N clear working days preceding the move: STGO art 3(3) leaves out Saturdays, Sundays and any GB public holiday (England and Wales, or Scotland, by the tenant's planning.abnormal_nation); Construction and Use leaves out Sundays and bank holidays only; a Special Order counts calendar days. The holidays are GOV.UK's bank-holidays.json (OGL), a copy shipped in backend/data/bank_holidays.json and a fresh one fetched on the Settings card into the config row bank_holidays.
  • Attendant, markers, speeds, escort, height. An attendant over 3.5 m wide or 18.75 m rigid (STGO Sch 6, C&U Sch 12); the side and end markers at 1 / 2 / 3 m projections (the lighting and marking code), speeds 60 / 50 / 40 mph for STGO Category 1 and lower for 2 and 3; an escort expected on motorways over 4.6 m wide or 130 t and on other roads over 4.1 m, 30.5 m or 100 t (National Highways' code of practice para 4.4: no law requires one; the police decide); over 3 m high the height is shown in the cab (C&U reg 10), over 4.95 m every bridge is a question, 5.486 m and 6.096 m are the signing thresholds. Police time bans are a per-tenant table (police_time_bans), empty by default.
  • The route (D67). POST /api/abnormal/{id}/route calls HERE Routing v8 with vehicle[grossWeight / weightPerAxle / height / width / length / axleCount] at the typed figures (HERE's maxima: 50 m high and wide, 30 m long — a longer load is drawn at 30 m and the row says so), return=polyline,summary,actions,instructions and spans=names, routeNumbers, functionalClass, length, notices, gate to gate, through the planner's via points, the return leg too when it is abnormal. Stored on the row: the line, HERE's actions (a severity word on turns, the exit number on roundabouts, "pass N roundabouts" only in the words), the roads in words, the signed-limit hits from road_limits at the load's size (limits.check_line), and the candidates in pinch_json.
  • The potential pinch points. Every HERE turn (its severity word) and roundabout exit, a slip road or keep for a long load (over 25.9 m, or 18.75 m rigid), a sharp bend in the line itself (60° within 40 m, none within 30 m of a HERE turn), any HERE notice, every signed limit the load is over or exactly at, and — from the community layer's second pass over the same Geofabrik extract (source osm_structures: bridge, railway, power, traffic_calming, barrier=height_restrictor and the maxheight / maxwidth / maxweight / maxaxleload / maxlength tags on roads) — a bridge or a tagged limit on the road the line follows (a way vertex within 12 m), a railway or an overhead line crossed (a segment meeting the line), a level crossing, calming or restrictor within 20 m; a tagged limit only when the load is over it or within 0.3 m / 10 % / 1 m. All Grey "to survey" (WSP's grades); a grade or note set on one survives a re-draw or a check when it is found again (matched on the OSM object, else the point and the words). POST /api/abnormal/{id}/structures looks again with no HERE call. OpenStreetMap is as complete as its mappers: a road may carry no tag.
  • Where it reads. GET /api/abnormal (the page), /summary (the Dashboard strip), the Look-ahead's abnormal block from abnormal.week(), the Gate arrivals markers from abnormal.gate_markers(), /api/abnormal/geojson (the staff map: a line a leg, a point a candidate), /api/public/abnormal (the public map, behind the map gate: approved, notified and moved movements only, the line and the words a visitor may read, no points; since 23 Oct 2026 read at page load and drawn in the month on screen only), the month figures (/api/public/month-kpis, one month or every month) carrying abnormal_loads, the ids of the public movements whose window touches the month, for the bar's count (each load once) and the map's month filter (abnormal.public_ids_by_month), /api/abnormal/calendar.xlsx|pdf (D73: nothing is submitted; the ESDAL details sheet of 22 Oct went on 27 Oct 2026 — the haulier applies in their own software and Wayscope keeps the references they give back).
  • Not modelled. Structure ratings (ESDAL and the owners), which forces and authorities a route crosses, swept paths, overhead clearances, indivisibility, drivers' hours for the escort crew, the weekly limits. Every screen says "advice"; none says "compliant".
  • The words on the screens (27 Oct 2026). The page prints one line, abnormal_rules.ADVICE_WORDS ("Advice from the typed figures. The haulier, ESDAL, the police and the road and bridge authorities decide."); the rules were read on 16 Oct 2026. The not-modelled list and the counted-apart rule (D70, D71) are in the user guide's chapter 14 and here; the Dashboard strip and the Look-ahead block say "Counted apart from the lorries' / the week's figures" and no screen prints a decision code.
  • What the page leaves out (28 Oct 2026; the user: no supplier or back-end words on a screen). HERE's turn severity word is kept in actions_json but not shown; a "pass N roundabouts" row says "straight over each" (HERE counts them only in its instruction words); a HERE notice shows its title, never its code or severity. T2 Routing with HERE

The screens say a route is routed or not routed yet. In the code and the data this is baking: a call to HERE Routing v8 with the vehicle's truck profile (gross weight, height, width, length, axles), stored in route_geometry per route, vehicle profile, leg and alternative. A stored route is what the screens call “cached geometry” in older notes.

  • Two legs. Every route is baked twice: laden out, empty back. The empty lorry is lighter, so it can use roads the loaded one legally cannot. A green vehicle chip means both legs are stored; amber means only the outbound is, which is enough to draw but not to work out a cycle time (the return is then assumed to mirror the outbound, and the screens say so). “Laden only” halves the HERE calls but leaves the return unbaked.
  • What clears a stored route. Moving a location or an end of a route, changing a gate, a zone set to avoid, or a haul road attached to the route. The app reports how many legs it clears and re-bakes them (one HERE call per leg; two for a spliced haul road). Moving a node re-bakes the routes it touches for the profiles ticked.
  • Gates. A route goes to a location's gate, else its access point, else the marker. A one-way gate is used only for the direction it serves. Changing a gate moves where HERE routes to, so the stored route has to be re-baked before the distances mean anything. A deactivated gate that a route selected makes that route refuse to bake, by name.
  • The hour a route is worked out for (29 Oct 2026, D94). Every bake (Route all, Route on a row, a way round) sends HERE departureTime = the next Tuesday at least two days ahead, 10:00 in the tenant's time zone (here_routing.bake_departure_time), resolved once per run; the run's answer carries it as departure_time. Until then no time was sent and every line was HERE’s “now” at the minute of the bake: on 28 Oct a Route all at 21:30 UTC stored roads 23–48 % longer on R001 and R010 than HERE gives at every weekday hour 06:00–18:00. The admin departure check (GET /api/admin/diagnostics/departure/{route}) replays the bake at chosen hours (the bake’s own hour added), names each option by the stored one it matches, and with plan=true makes no HERE call.
  • Busy-hour times (29 Oct 2026, task 0f-2). hour_times.py: an admin run (POST /api/admin/hour-times/clear, then /bake looped; GET /plan gives the cost) asks HERE for each (route, leg) of the Artic Tipper at 06:00, 08:00, 09:00, 12:00, 15:00, 17:00 and 18:00 on the bake’s Tuesday, exactly as the bake asks (network.leg_request, the permanent zones, three options): one call each. Every option is stored in route_hour_times with its line’s fingerprint and the STORED option with the same line (alt_index, NULL = a road not stored) and that line’s computed_at; a read trusts a row only while the stored line is still that one, so a re-route retires the times. hour_times.attach adds hours (cycle = both legs at that hour + the row’s turnaround) to the route analysis; another lorry type on the same road (both legs within 50 m) is scaled by the tipper’s hour-to-pinned ratio. Never in the plan’s figures. The collision evidence draws the tipper’s cycle at every hour (evidence_block), the quicker option marked.
  • How HERE ranks a lorry route, and why Wayscope keeps its order (29 Oct 2026). What we send: transportMode=truck with the vehicle's weight, height, width, length and axles, alternatives=2 (three options), the pinned departureTime (#t2-bake-hour) and no routingMode, so HERE uses its default, fast (the Routing API v8 reference, calculate routes). What HERE documents: fast optimises for travel time, but its own reference warns that the route it returns “may not be the route with the fastest possible travel time”: it may, for example, keep to a highway even when a quicker way exists. For lorries, HERE's truck routing guide says the truck mode prefers high-priority roads to low-priority ones (it names residential roads, small forest roads and narrow side streets, and staying on a highway rather than cutting through a city centre when the gain is minor), and that these preferences are adjusted over time. The reference calls the first route the optimal one and the others alternatives “aside from” it. What HERE does not publish: the weights it gives each road class, how it trades them against minutes, or how it orders the alternatives among themselves. So the exact ranking cannot be reproduced; it is HERE's. What HERE does not claim: nowhere does HERE say its first route is the safer or the lower-carbon one. What its documents support is a route legal for the lorry's size that prefers main roads. Main roads can mean more distance, so more carbon by the per-km factor: on R049 HERE's first option is about twice the distance of its third. Wayscope therefore never describes HERE's choice as safer or greener. It shows each option's distance, carbon, busy-hour times, collisions, cycle routes, left turns and the schools and homes near it, so the planner can see the trade-off (GET /api/admin/route-options/compare, route_compare.py). Measured on the demo, 29 Oct 2026 (50 routes, 41 with an alternative, 68 comparisons of HERE's first option with an alternative it gave, Artic Tipper, collisions 2021–2025 within 25 m): HERE's first option was the shorter in 49, the quicker in 55 and the lower-carbon in 49 (3,793 against 4,160 kg CO₂e a trip summed over the pairs). Collisions largely follow the distance. Where it was the shorter, it had fewer collisions in 39 of 49. Where HERE chose the longer road (19), it had more collisions in 15, though on R002 and R041 the shorter option it passed over carried about 2.6 times the collisions. So the counts support “usually the shorter, quicker, lower-carbon route” and do not support “safer” on their own. Schools and homes are not compared yet: on the deployment the community layer is fetched along the main routes and diversions only, so an alternative's counts would be low. Restrictions are different: legal limits apply with the truck mode, and physical ones (weight, height, axle load) because the vehicle's size is sent; a road the lorry may not use is avoided or comes back with a notices entry. A preference produces no notice. What Wayscope does: alt_index 0 is HERE's first route and is the Primary every figure uses; nothing re-ranks by time or km, except that a dated zone's diversion picks the shortest stored option that misses it (#t2-diversions). Make primary (network.promote_alternative) swaps an option in until the next re-route. Measured on live, 29 Oct: after the busy-hour run, two of the 50 demo routes had a stored option shorter and quicker at every hour than the Primary: R049 Alt 2 laden 19.7 km / 23.5 min against 43.0 km / 47.2 min, and R036 Alt 1 on its return (about 3 km and 3 min). GET /api/admin/diagnostics/route/{id}?probe=true showed no notices on any of their options, so the likely cause is HERE's road-class preference. That is not verified: the road-class diagnostic reads only HERE's first option.
  • Alternatives. HERE may return more than one option per vehicle. “Make primary” swaps an alternative into first place (alt_index 0); re-baking restores HERE's ranking.
  • Zones. A zone ticked to avoid and with NO dates is sent to HERE as an avoid-area (the rectangle around what was drawn) with every bake, and every baked leg crossing it is cleared and re-routed. Legs baked before zones existed are matched approximately: there is no record of what they avoided. Unticking Active retires a zone and keeps the record of why past bakes went the way they did.
  • Zones with dates: diversions (28 Oct 2026). A dated routing zone is never sent with the main line's bake (network.active_avoid takes undated zones only; until 28 Oct the zones in force on the day of the bake shaped the line for every month). Instead route_choice.refresh_zones() — run after every zone save, Route all, route bake, Make primary and way round, geometry only, no HERE call — cuts the programme at every dated zone's start and end and, for each (route, vehicle profile, leg) whose alt-0 line crosses a zone in force (exact test on the drawn shape, zones.line_hits_geometry), stores a route_choice row: the SHORTEST stored option that misses every routing zone in force then (in_use), or needs_route. “Find a way round” (POST /api/admin/route-diversions/way-round, admin) bakes ONE option per such leg with the zones' areas added to avoid[areas], stored at alt_index 10 (route_choice.WAY_ROUND_BASE) with the zone ids in route_geometry.avoids; a plain re-bake keeps it; a failure writes nothing; a returned line that still crosses the zone is no_way_round. “Keep the main route” is a keep_main row the refresh honours (declined). Readers: derived.cycle_for(month_index, week_index) blends each option by its share of the period's working days (Mon–Fri, bank holidays not removed), trips a lorry a day the fewest of the options used; route_analysis(pairs=) pairs a laden and an empty option; the month figures, costlines, carbon and Look-ahead go through it; the NO_WAY_ROUND flag; lorry limits are checked on options in use and count in the weeks their dates touch; /api/public/map-data carries Diversion features (months, periods, zone names) that the map swaps into the leg in those months; the route sheet adds a page per diversion. Since 29 Oct 2026 (PR B) the rest follows too: the shared-road index (road_sections.polylines_for) and the council clip (areas.compute) also hold every option in use, keyed R012@1 (route_choice.leg_key); a stretch only a route's main line and its own diversion share is not a shared road (owners counted by route); road_load(month) and the council month give each key its share of the month's working days (route_choice.month_weights) and list routes, not keys; the bulletin, export and CLP plan maps take the month's diversion (bulletin_doc._geometries(month_index)); the Look-ahead week map and its PDF the week's (lookahead.week_geometry(days), the option used on most of the week's days); the Simulation's legs the period's (sim._legs_for(days)); a collision near an option in use is along a route (collisions.along(in_use)); the community strip covers the options in use (a Fetch again picks them up). The indexes are rebuilt when the set of options in use changes. The table route_choice carries hours_from / hours_to and reason_kind for task 0d's peak-hour choice (offered until accepted).
  • Haul roads. Splice assumes HERE has never heard of the road: route to one end, use the drawn line, route on from the other end (two HERE calls per leg). Via lets HERE find its own way through (one call). Which end is the entry is worked out per leg from the geometry, so the order the road was drawn in does not matter. HERE will not accept a custom speed, so the haul stretch's time is substituted afterwards. A leg that runs along a haul road comes back as ONE option (alternatives on each sub-call cannot be ranked against each other), so there is nothing to make primary on it. A gate's access road (the bellmouth from the public road to the gate) is the exception: when a leg's only drawn roads are the access roads at its two ends, the one HERE call between them asks for alternatives and each is spliced between the same two access roads.
  • Restrictions. Checked against the stored route when it is baked and refreshed on demand; the check reports what lies within its distance of the baked line, not that it is on your carriageway. The Look-ahead says which providers were not checked since baking.
  • Cost. Each bake spends HERE calls. Since 22 Oct 2026 the confirm dialogs no longer count them (the words sweep): one call a leg, two for a spliced haul road, both legs per vehicle unless Laden only.
  • The cycle source. cycle_source is here (both legs baked) or here_return_estimated (the return taken as the outbound; the screens mark it “return estimated” and ‡). The screens used to print “HERE” / “HERE‡”.
  • Average haul speed (planning.avg_haul_speed_kmh) is read only by the public map's estimate for a route with no baked drive time.
  • Haul roads and the avoid list. A haul road is never sent as an avoid-area; routes attached to it are sent through it. HERE's own time for a spliced stretch is kept beside the substituted one, so the change is auditable.
  • Moving a route's ends. Its forecast lines (and their month rows) and Look-ahead weeks keep the route id and now describe the new haul; the stored route for each vehicle is cleared and baked again.
  • Zones in the panel. The zone panel prints the exact rectangle that will be sent before saving.

T7 Sourced and assumed figures, live sources, and what is not finished

Moved here from the user guide’s Appendix D on 22 Oct 2026 (the user: no back-end detail in the user guide). It answers “where does that number come from?” in a meeting.

How a haulage number is made

cycle          = drive out (laden) + drive back (tare) + turnaround
trips per day  = the most round trips in a driver's day: n × cycle + the required break ≤ the shift
                 (at most 13 h), and n × driving ≤ 9 h  (driving = out + back + gate to face;
                 break 45 min once driving passes 4½ h or work 9 h, else 30 min once work passes
                 6 h; the 45 may be taken as 15 + 30). 0 = not one round trip fits: one lorry a trip
trips needed   = ceil(tonnes / payload)
vehicles       = ceil(movements per day / trips per day)

Both drive legs are routed separately — the return at tare weight, so the way back is not assumed to be the way out reversed and different weight limits can apply. Turnaround is the vehicle's own load and unload minutes, plus gate induction, plus either gate-to-face time or a drawn haul road — never both.

Trips are not vehicles

A trip is one loaded run; a vehicle makes several a day. A fleet figure worked out as tonnes ÷ payload alone is a trip count, and overstates the fleet by whatever that multiple is — typically three to six times.

The live sources, and how they fail

  • Lorry routing — every routed distance and drive time. Its road graph lags the physical road, which on a construction programme matters: roads are realigned for years, and a route routed before a realignment can follow a road that is no longer there.
  • Road restrictions (where a provider is connected for the country) — kept for a short while, and stored per route with the date it was checked. Mass, height and width give a real verdict; a weak bridge gives a load class, not a tonnage, so it is reported unjudged. The whole layer is advisory. The source carries expired records. The provider's data is read at most every 30 minutes. When a refresh cannot reach the provider the stored result stays in use and the Look-ahead says so (“could not reach …; showing the last check”).
  • National Highways closures on the public map (27 Oct 2026). The map asks for the route check (GET /api/routes/restrictions) on every page load; the answer is now kept in memory (read_cache) and rebuilt only when the feed is replaced, a route is written or the day turns. The feed itself is fetched at most every 30 minutes, and a background refresh fetches it every 20 minutes so a visitor never waits for it. How stale a closure can get: normally at most about 30 minutes after National Highways changes it. When a fetch fails, fails on any page, or comes back with no closures at all (National Highways’ own service answered zero records on 27 Oct 2026 after a republish) while a good copy no more than 24 hours old is held, that copy is served marked stale and the provider is asked again after 5 minutes; past 24 hours the empty or failed answer is accepted. The copy lives in one process and goes with a restart. POST /api/admin/restrictions/refresh drops the 30-minute copy and keeps the last good one; GET /api/admin/diagnostics/restrictions shows the feed’s age and whether it is stale. The screens say nothing about any of this.
  • Diesel price — for EU countries and Great Britain, refreshed in the background, never on the page read. A failed fetch keeps the last good figure and says when it failed. Elsewhere there is nothing to fetch: the price is typed.
  • Loading an organisation — an admin imports a whole organisation as one package file (POST /api/admin/tenant/import, into an empty organisation) and loads the map overlay (PUT /api/admin/overlay); GET /api/admin/tenant/export writes the organisation back out as one file. After an import, bake the network: imported routes read “not routed yet” until then.
  • Basemaps and Street View — optional. With no key the popups simply show no photo. With a key the map asks Google's Street View metadata first and fetches a picture only where one exists within 50 m, so no picture request is paid for where there is none.
  • The PDF's map — drawn from the Mapbox static image service. When it cannot be reached the PDF draws a schematic instead and its caption says why; the sheet is otherwise identical.
What leaves the platform

Normal operation sends site and route coordinates to the routing, mapping and restriction services, and a coordinate to Street View where it is used. No quantity, rate or forecast leaves. This is inherent to any routed map product — the alternative is not having routing — but it is a fair question and better answered before it is asked.
