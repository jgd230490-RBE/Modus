Technical guide

For the people who run Wayscope and answer “where does that number come from?”. The user guide says what a screen means and how to use it; the info boxes (ⓘ) on the screens say what each figure means in a sentence. This page holds what the screens leave out: the formulas in code terms, the reads behind each screen, how routing is stored, and the settings that live on the deployment.

The words on the screens

Since 13 Oct 2026 a screen does not say API, endpoint, server, JSON, cache, geometry, bake or token (except the admin token's own field, the admin panel behind Config → More, the “Everything (JSON)” tab and the Diagnostics panel). Those words are used here. T6 maps the old screen words to the new ones.

T1 How each figure is made

Every planning figure on the Dashboard comes from one read, GET /api/costing/lines, which uses the same functions as the Look-ahead (backend/costlines.py). The browser formats; it does not work anything out. A forecast month (the screens used to say line-month) is one month of one forecast line: a route, a team, a section, a material and one vehicle.

Tonnes and conversions

The planned quantity of every forecast month in view, in tonnes through each line's conversion (m³ and vehicle loads included). The Dashboard card shows a compact figure (bigNum, e.g. 12.3k t); its info box shows the exact figure.

Trips and deliveries a day

trips (per forecast month)  = ceil(tonnes ÷ planning payload)        — rounded up per line-month
deliveries a day            = Σ trips ÷ (months in view × working days per month)

Working days per month is planning.working_days_per_month on Config → Planning (22 by default). Each delivery is a two-way movement. The Look-ahead and the Today panel use the same rule per day.

Distance and the cycle

lorry-km      = trips × the routed (baked) route distance, both legs, for the line's vehicle
cycle         = drive out (laden) + drive back (empty) + turnaround
turnaround    = the vehicle's load + unload minutes + gate induction + (gate-to-face OR a drawn haul road, never both)

The per-trip distance is on the route's basis: the round trip unless the route says loaded only. An unbaked line (no stored route for its vehicle) has no distance: it adds nothing to the km, CO₂e and fair totals, which say how many lines they exclude. It is excluded, never estimated. The load and unload minutes are the vehicle's own (Config → Vehicles, seeded from backend/factors.json); blank minutes use the planning defaults.

Vehicles and peak fleet

trips per vehicle-day = floor(shift hours ÷ cycle)
vehicles (per line)   = trips per day ÷ trips per vehicle-day      — the server's `vehicles`
peak fleet            = the largest same-month sum of vehicles needed across the lines in view

That is trips ÷ (working days × what one vehicle does in a shift). An unbaked line has no cycle, so it needs an unknown number of vehicles and is left out of the sum. Trips are not vehicles: a fleet worked out as tonnes ÷ payload alone is a trip count.

Delivered against plan

delivered t        = Σ the week actuals typed on Look-ahead → Account, through each line's payload (actual_tonnes)
delivered %        = delivered t ÷ planned t across every forecast month in view
on reported months = delivered t ÷ planned t on the forecast months that carry an actual

A day actual rolls into its week. A week or month nobody reported counts as nothing (not yet delivered); it is never assumed. The Today panel's “this week to date” counts line-days (one day of one line).

Carbon

CO₂e (t)      = km × the vehicle's per-km factor ÷ 1000
              — DESNZ 2026 kg CO₂e per vehicle-km (average laden, both legs, from Config)
kg CO₂e per t = tCO₂e × 1000 ÷ the tonnes on baked lines
tonne-km      = tonnes × the route's basis km, baked lines

The factor is set per vehicle on Config → Vehicles and stored per km whatever unit the tenant reads (miles are converted for display only). Tailpipe only, one average-laden factor on both legs. The Carbon planner's all-in figure (materials and waste as well) is a different total: see the user guide, section 6.

Planned cost

Planned £ = the route's typed rates (summed: per load, per tonne and per km), or the Config target rate where the route has none, marked “target”. A route with any rate of its own is priced from that alone; the two are never mixed. Per tonne, per trip and per km are the same total divided by the tonnes, trips and km the lines run.

Fair cost (the model)

fair / trip = fuel (litres from the vehicle class, tonnes carried and the live diesel index)
            + cycle hours × (driver + vehicle standing)
            + km × running
            × (1 + margin)

The coefficients are on Config → Costing · fuel. Winter months add the fair_price.winter_consumption_uplift_pct fuel uplift (8 % by default). The diesel index is the government weekly price for Great Britain or the EU Weekly Oil Bulletin for an EU country, refreshed in the background, or typed. With no index there is no fair price. Shown on every routed line as “fair (model)”: a floor for negotiation, never a quote, never mixed with a typed rate. Three coefficients have no public source and are labelled as assumptions. The starting coefficients come from factors.json: fair_price_by_country holds a set per country (Great Britain, in £), and fair_price is the set for every other organisation (an Estonian set, in EUR). A coefficient missing from the organisation's own block is read from its country's set, and the page offers the country's set in one click when the figures differ.

Gate arrivals

Read from GET /api/gates/arrivals?month=N (backend/gate_arrivals.py). The same trips rule, all teams summed at the gate a lorry arrives by: laden at the destination's entry gate, empty (coming to load) at the origin's. Arrivals a day = trips ÷ working days. The gap is the shift's minutes ÷ arrivals, an average with no hourly peak. Red only when that gap is shorter than the gate's typed check-in; a gate with no check-in typed is never compared. A team code gets the totals and the team names, never another team's figure.

Since 14 Oct 2026 (backend/gate_hours.py, the one rule for every screen and export): a gate's open minutes are its typed open hours (a window that ends before it starts runs past midnight) less the scheme's no-delivery peak bands (planning.peak_bands, Config → Planning) inside them; a gate with no hours typed uses the shift's minutes, and the peak bands cannot be taken off it (the shift has no clock times). Capacity a day = open minutes ÷ check-in minutes a lorry; the gap uses the same open minutes. Two steps: Queue likely (amber) at 85 % of capacity or more (NEAR_PCT), Over gate capacity (red) past it — the red rule above, renamed on screen. Open days (seven flags, Monday first) are tested on the Look-ahead's dated days only: a month figure is a working-day average. The reply carries near_per_day (85 % of capacity) for the chart's guide tick.

Stockpiles

balance at the week's end = opening + typed week actuals delivered there − typed consumption

A week nobody reported counts as nothing. Consumption is typed on Look-ahead → Account. The chart is red past capacity and amber from 90 %; a stockpile with no capacity recorded is listed, never drawn as zero.

CLP monitoring

Read from GET /api/clp/monitoring?month=N (backend/clp.py), the first time the Dashboard section is opened; the CLP pack prints the same reply. Each chart comes back with state "set", or "empty" and missing in words. Every figure is the module's that owns it, never worked out again:

routes vs cap      bulletin.route_cap_rows: the month's approved loads ÷ working days vs max_vehicles_per_day
sites vs CLP cap   gate_arrivals.location_visits: laden + coming-to-load trips ÷ working days vs clp_cap_per_day
vehicle / Euro     carbon.scenario's baseline lines: trips by vehicle (equal to costlines' trips), Euro class from Config
councils           areas.area_load: districts, movements a day, laden and total lorry-km (÷ 1.609344 for miles)
carbon a tonne     all-in total_t × 1000 ÷ tonnes, as planned and with the target's levers; target_t (carbon_target.status) ÷ plan tonnes
trips avoided      the same scenario: baseline trips − trips with the target's levers; delta_vkm_km

The CLP pack is the same reply printed: GET /api/clp/pack.pdf?month=N and /api/clp/pack.xlsx?month=N (backend/clp_pack.py), exactly the charts in state "set", each with its table, basis and source; the rest listed under "Not drawn this month". It draws and never computes. The demo notice is on every page (the shared page frame) and heads every sheet.

Scope: the vehicle, carbon and trip charts are the caller's visible lines (a team code: its own, and its share of the target); the caps and councils are the scheme's. No figure is observed: no gate log is held (decision D42).

T2 Routing with HERE

The screens say a route is routed or not routed yet. In the code and the data this is baking: a call to HERE Routing v8 with the vehicle's truck profile (gross weight, height, width, length, axles), stored in route_geometry per route, vehicle profile, leg and alternative. A stored route is what the screens call “cached geometry” in older notes.

  • Two legs. Every route is baked twice: laden out, empty back. The empty lorry is lighter, so it can use roads the loaded one legally cannot. A green vehicle chip means both legs are stored; amber means only the outbound is, which is enough to draw but not to work out a cycle time (the return is then assumed to mirror the outbound, and the screens say so). “Laden only” halves the HERE calls but leaves the return unbaked.
  • What clears a stored route. Moving a location or an end of a route, changing a gate, a zone set to avoid, or a haul road attached to the route. The app reports how many legs it clears and re-bakes them (one HERE call per leg; two for a spliced haul road). Moving a node re-bakes the routes it touches for the profiles ticked.
  • Gates. A route goes to a location's gate, else its access point, else the marker. A one-way gate is used only for the direction it serves. Changing a gate moves where HERE routes to, so the stored route has to be re-baked before the distances mean anything. A deactivated gate that a route selected makes that route refuse to bake, by name.
  • Alternatives. HERE may return more than one option per vehicle. “Make primary” swaps an alternative into first place (alt_index 0); re-baking restores HERE's ranking.
  • Zones. A zone ticked to avoid is sent to HERE as an avoid-area (the rectangle around what was drawn) and every baked leg crossing it is cleared and re-routed. Legs baked before zones existed are matched approximately: there is no record of what they avoided. Unticking Active retires a zone and keeps the record of why past bakes went the way they did.
  • Haul roads. Splice assumes HERE has never heard of the road: route to one end, use the drawn line, route on from the other end (two HERE calls per leg). Via lets HERE find its own way through (one call). Which end is the entry is worked out per leg from the geometry, so the order the road was drawn in does not matter. HERE will not accept a custom speed, so the haul stretch's time is substituted afterwards.
  • Restrictions. Checked against the stored route when it is baked and refreshed on demand; the check reports what lies within its distance of the baked line, not that it is on your carriageway. The Look-ahead says which providers were not checked since baking.
  • Cost. Each bake spends HERE calls; the confirm dialogs say how many before anything runs.

T4 Settings and deployment

  • Sign-in (G3, 14 Oct 2026). A login is a row in users (email, name, role team with one of the six internal team slots, planner or admin, a scrypt hash, a disabled flag; kind staff or demo). POST /api/auth/signin makes a server-side session: a random token in the wayscope_session cookie (HttpOnly, Secure, SameSite=Lax; 12 h, or 30 days with "keep me signed in"), stored only as its SHA-256 in user_sessions. Sign out and Disable delete the rows, so the cookie dies everywhere at once. A session resolves to the same access dict a code does (access.resolve_request: a valid X-Access-Code first, else the cookie), so every line filter is unchanged. Invite (72 h) and reset (60 min) links are single-use, sent as APP_URL/#invite=… or #reset=… (a fragment: never in a server log or a Referer) and stored hashed in auth_tokens. Limits: 5 failed sign-ins per email per 15 min, 30 per IP; 3 reset requests per email per hour, 10 per IP; kept in auth_attempts. The tables carry tenant_id but are not package data: a package import leaves them alone.
  • The shared codes (IPT1_CODE…IPT6_CODE, PLANNER_CODE, ADMIN_CODE) work only while set; deleting them on Render ends them (decided for 31 Oct 2026). The sign-in screen offers the code form only while /api/auth/me says codes: true.
  • Writes from the app. The app's one fetch wrapper adds X-Wayscope: 1. A write that the session cookie authenticates without that header is refused (403), and so is any /api/auth/* POST: another site cannot add a header. CORS answers no other origin.
  • The audit trail. The middleware writes one audit_log row for every non-GET /api/ request that answers 2xx: who (the login; "<role> code (shared)"; or "admin token"), when, the path, the query and the JSON body with every key ending in password / token / code / secret removed, capped at 4 KB. POSTs that change nothing (the costing preview, the carbon scenario and its PDF) are not logged; the sign-in calls log their own events. Demo logins also log page opens (POST /api/activity). Read it on Users → Activity or GET /api/admin/audit.csv.
  • Demo leads (HU14). A demo request makes (or renews) a demo login on the team slot DEMO_TEAM_CODE_VAR names, for 14 days, and emails its personal link (#demo=…), reusable until the login ends. The leads CSV gains login, expiry, sign-ins, first and last seen, pages.
  • The admin token. Admin actions (saving Configuration, changing routes, zones and gates, the fuel index, Users) pass on an admin LOGIN, or with the token typed in the admin token field (Config → More, the Data pages' header, the fuel widget, Users); it is remembered in this browser. It is checked against ADMIN_TOKEN on Render. Since 12 Oct 2026 an unset ADMIN_TOKEN refuses those actions (it used to open them). The Data → Locations note now points at the field in the page header (it used to name a bake panel that no longer exists).
  • Routing key. Routing needs HERE_API_KEY in Render → Environment for the modus-web service. Without it the Data → Routes banner says routing is not set up, and nothing bakes.
  • Configuration. The live source is the configuration table; backend/factors.json is the reset-to-default (Config → More → Reset to file). Vehicle ids are the keys forecast lines and stored routes are keyed on, so they cannot be renamed, and a vehicle the network is baked for cannot be removed. Routing dimensions and notes are edited on the “Everything (JSON)” tab.
  • Clearing every route (Data → Routes) deletes every route and its stored geometry; locations are kept. It was built to retire the first seed (the V2 seed).
  • Diagnostics (Data → Routes) shows each vehicle profile against factors.json: ● it appears in stored geometry; ⚠ the name is not a key in factors.json and falls back to _default (identical payload and CO₂ for every such profile, which looks like the vehicle being ignored).

T8 The community considerations layer

Staff only (14 Oct 2026). GET /api/community serves what is stored as one GeoJSON FeatureCollection with the kinds, each source's date and the credit line; GET /api/community/status the same without the features. Both answer 401 to the map password's cookie and to no credential. POST /api/community/refresh (a planner or an admin; 403 for a team code) starts a fetch on a thread: a few minutes, never on a page view.

The strip is 250 m each side of every stored route line (both legs, every vehicle) and 500 m round every site; a feature is kept when any part of it is inside, and its distance is stored. T9 The collision history (CLOCS v5 C1)

Staff only (16 Oct 2026). GET /api/collisions serves what is stored as one GeoJSON FeatureCollection (year and month, never the day or time) with the years held and the credit line; GET /api/collisions/status the same without the features. Both answer 401 to the map password's cookie and to no credential. POST /api/collisions/refresh (a planner or an admin; 403 for a team code) starts a fetch on a thread. GET /api/routes/{id}/collision-evidence.pdf and .xlsx give one route's evidence (staff; a team code its own team's routes only, 404 for another's). The plan endpoints take collisions=1 (off by default).

QuestionAnswer (checked 16 Oct 2026)
SourceGOV.UK “Road safety open data” (DfT STATS19), Great Britain from 1979. Three CSV tables a year, dft-road-casualty-statistics-{collision,vehicle,casualty}-{year}.csv on data.dft.gov.uk, joined on collision_index.
LicenceOpen Government Licence v3.0; the credit line names DfT road safety data.
Years readThe latest five FINAL years. Final data for a year comes out in late July of the next; from August the newest year is last year, before it the year before. If the newest file is not published yet (HTTP 404) the five years step back one and the page says so. Provisional mid-year data is not read (DfT calls it unvalidated).
How it is readEach table downloaded whole (the server refuses HEAD and ignores Range requests; about 19–21 MB a table a year, about 250 MB for five years), read row by row, and deleted before the next, so the disk holds one file at a time. Collisions outside the strip's box are dropped before any distance is worked out; vehicles and casualties are read only for the collisions kept. All or nothing: a failure keeps what was stored.
The stripThe community layer's: 250 m each side of every stored road route (both legs, every vehicle, and the stored alternatives, each under its own key) and 500 m round every site. Each collision keeps its distance to each route it is near. “On the route” = within 25 m (on the 2025 data the count flattens after 20 m; 25 m allows for a dual carriageway). Places = two or more collisions within 50 m of each other (single linkage), ordered along the primary loaded leg. Each collision is marked along = route (near a primary route or a site) or alternative (near a stored alternative only). The map draws the alternatives' only with the Alternative routes layer; the counts and the plans use route only and count the others apart (alternatives_only).
CodesSeverity 1 fatal, 2 serious, 3 slight (KSI = 1 or 2). Vulnerable road users (the CLOCS v5 definition): pedestrian (casualty_class 3), cyclist (type 1), motorcyclist (2, 3, 4, 5, 23, 97), horse rider (16), e-scooter or powered transporter (33, or escooter_flag 1), mobility scooter (22). Goods vehicle over 3.5 t: vehicle type 20, 21 or 98. Junction from junction_detail (0 = not at or within 20 m of a junction).
SeverityAs the police reported it. DfT's adjusted figures (for forces not using injury-based reporting) are not used; the caveat on every output says severity recording changed after 2016.

The rows live per organisation (collision_records, collision_meta) and travel in its package. The words on every output: “police-reported injury collisions”, the period beside every count, and “evidence for a route risk assessment, not the assessment”. No score, rate, ranking or colour status.
